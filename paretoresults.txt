That means the story isn't "upscaling doesn't help" anymore. It genuinely does help. Suggested wording (revised from what you asked for)
Don't use "the trade does not materialize" — that's false post-fix. Use something like:

"Running the released baseline checkpoint at higher test-time resolution does improve boundary IoU (0.358 → 0.381 from 0.75× to native resolution), confirming that resolution is a real, usable knob for this architecture. However, at matched FLOPs cost, our method exceeds this upscaling curve by 0.015–0.037 boundary IoU (r=1) — including a comparison against the most expensive point we measured (native resolution, 177 GFLOPs), which HI1 still beats using 40% fewer FLOPs (106 GFLOPs). We report FLOPs as the primary cost metric since it is deterministic and reproducible; wall-clock measurements on our hardware (a thermally-limited T4) show a consistent pattern but a wider noise floor."

This keeps your "I1 is Pareto-dominant by construction" point completely intact (it's the cleanest sentence in the paper — zero architecture change, zero cost, +0.020 boundary IoU, not contingent on any of this scale-sweep analysis) and reframes the BRH/HI1 story as genuine FLOPs-efficiency rather than "no free lunch exists," which is the more defensible and frankly more interesting claim to a reviewer.

One honest gap to flag: still n=2 baseline replicates for the scale curve (not n=3), same limitation as before — the third replicate isn't on disk in this checkout. Doesn't change any conclusion here given the margins involved, but worth a footnote if a reviewer asks about it.
